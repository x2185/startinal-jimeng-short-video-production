# High-density continuous story

Use this route for a user-requested 20–30 second, fast-moving product story where causal continuity matters more than deterministic clip-by-clip retakes. It is a Dreamina CLI route, not a replacement for short independent clips when fine product operations need strict control.

## Route selection

Use one continuous validation candidate when all of these are true:

- the user asks for a quick, plot-led sequence with frequent visual changes;
- the current `dreamina multimodal2video -h` confirms that the chosen model accepts the requested duration;
- product identity can be locked by a role-labelled same-SKU reference pack within the selected model's live input limit; and
- the story can avoid unsupported precise mechanics and readable model-generated text, while any payment storyline is grounded in a seller-confirmed holder configuration and the user's own tap-enabled card.

Use short linked clips instead if the selected model does not accept the requested finished duration, or if the product must visibly open, load, insert, fasten, connect, dispense, or otherwise change state in a precise way. Keep the **same 25-second causal beat map** and choose model-supported source durations that sum to 25 seconds; do not replace it with disconnected five-second showcases. A cardholder-and-tap beat may remain in the continuous story when the holder configuration is well referenced; do not describe the holder as the payment instrument.

## Linked-clip adaptation

For each source clip, record: its exact duration; the incoming state; one dominant action; the outgoing stable state; the tail-frame contract (composition, pose, product orientation, props, lighting, camera); and the transition mechanism. A good join is one a human editor can cut cleanly even if the two AI clips do not share pixels: a hand or object passing the lens, a door/counter crossing, a completed glance followed by a response, a pan ending on a foreground object, or a deliberate ordinary cut on cause-and-effect.

Do not use a mid-tap, mid-insertion, mid-turn, or half-spoken line as a join. Generate and approve the earlier clip first, extract a sharp stable handoff frame, and use it together with the fixed product reference for the next clip whenever the selected model accepts multiple references.

## Beat design

Write one causal spine before adding visual variety:

`goal/problem → pressure → decision → safe product moment → turn → emotional/result payoff`.

Then assign 1–3-second microbeats, and use 0.5–1-second sub-beats when a supplied user script demonstrates that level of direction. Each row needs exactly one dominant actor action or reaction; inside the row, state the camera's first subject, ordered sub-actions, eye-line/hand position, and exit movement. A practical 25-second shape is:

| Time | Story job | Direction rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3s | Immediate hook and goal | Establish a readable problem or intention in the first second. |
| 3–7s | Pressure | Use one motivated movement, not an unrelated location jump. |
| 7–11s | Decision | Bring the product into the action without concealing its identity. |
| 11–16s | Progress | Use follow, push-in, or match action to retain spatial logic. |
| 16–20s | Turn | A reaction or environmental change makes the outcome feel earned. |
| 20–23s | Product proof | Include a sharp 2-second close shot with the product large and unobscured. |
| 23–25s | Payoff | Settle into a stable product-visible ending frame. |

Quick cutting is a consequence of movement and decisions. Use only motivated transitions: object or hand passing close to lens, a character entering/exiting through a doorway, a pan/follow movement, a foreground occlusion, or a clear ordinary cut. Do not tell the model to make arbitrary edits every second.

## Variant design

Let the user's current brief determine the premise, tone, audience, prohibited content, and the creative elements that must remain. When the user supplies a script or detailed prompt, use its plot, beat order, relationship, dialogue intent, visual moments, and ending as the base version; optional route libraries may suggest alternatives but must not override it. For a requested creative high-density variant set, change at least three of these between variants: the opening disruption, character goal, relationship, pressure, setting, proof order, turn, outcome, camera logic, and final emotional beat. Keep each variant's product action grounded in current evidence.

Every variant needs its own causal spine and 1–3-second beat map. A new wardrobe, background, or adjective does not make a new story. Avoid repeating the same reveal, disbelief reaction, action sequence, or payoff unless the user explicitly asks for systematic A/B variants.

## Optional narrative engine: skeptic-to-convert

Use this family-friendly UGC engine when the product has a seller-confirmed, visually demonstrable use and the current brief calls for an intergenerational or household reaction story. It is an abstract structure only: derive the relationships, places, dialogue, proof action, and payoff from the current product and user brief.

`skeptical discovery → owner defends/explains → in-context test → skeptic's attitude changes → relationship payoff`.

| Time share | Story function | Direction rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–20% | Skeptical discovery | A believable authority figure finds or notices the unusual item and gives one concise, grounded reaction. |
| 20–35% | Owner's response | The owner protects, explains, or commits to showing the real use; preserve a readable product view. |
| 35–55% | Proof setup | Move directly to the product-relevant context with a motivated transition; omit routine setup. |
| 55–70% | Demonstrable beat | Show one evidence-backed action or result without combining several fine operations. |
| 70–85% | Reversal | Let the skeptic react, reconsider, or adopt the new viewpoint through an understated response. |
| 85–100% | Relationship payoff | Resolve through an earned household, friendship, or family moment with a stable product-visible ending. |

For a 15–20 second version, compress the engine into one action and one payoff. For 25 seconds, give the reversal and relationship payoff room to land. Set one final duration before drafting; do not use a prompt whose declared duration conflicts with its time-coded beats.

## Prompt package

The director prompt must include:

- product passport and exact no-substitution instruction;
- character/wardrobe/location continuity anchors;
- time-coded microbeats with action, camera, product position, and transition;
- the final stable frame; and
- targeted negative constraints: changed SKU or colour, duplicate product, warped geometry, malformed hands, unrelated brands, unreadable or invented text, and unsupported action.

Keep spoken dialogue short, optional, and separate from delicate interaction. Put captions, titles, sound effects, and exact cut timing in deterministic post-production rather than asking the model to render them.

## Acceptance gate

Accept a candidate only if all of these pass:

- the story has a readable cause-and-effect chain, not merely attractive rapid shots;
- the product remains the same SKU, colour, shape, parts, and scale throughout;
- the product proof close shot is sharp and unobscured for at least two seconds;
- transitions are motivated and do not teleport people, product, or wardrobe; and
- hands, faces, labels, and geometry are intact.

If causal flow fails, revise the affected microbeats before another paid attempt. If identity or a fine action fails, reduce that action or route it to verified real footage; do not hide the defect in editing.
