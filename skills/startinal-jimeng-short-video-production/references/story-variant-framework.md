# Flexible product creative framework

Use this reference only when the user asks for variants or has not supplied a detailed script that selects a single route. Derive each route from the current product brief; do not carry over a previous product's story, props, characters, or actions.

## Creative modes

Select only modes supported by the current product facts and evidence.

| Mode | Story engine | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Situation story | An ordinary goal, pressure, turn, and payoff. | A believable adult context and product-relevant outcome fit the brief. |
| Function proof | One confirmed use changes the situation. | The product has a visible, evidence-backed action or result. |
| Visual reveal | A visual question resolves through appearance or detail. | Shape, material, colour, or configuration is the strength. |
| Social reaction | Adults observe, compare, guess, or react. | A real interaction can support the product proof. |
| Challenge or test | A simple visible goal is attempted. | The product has a confirmed action or behaviour. |
| Gift, collection, or lifestyle | The product changes a real display, packing, giving, or daily moment. | The relevant product role is confirmed. |

## User-led route selection

When the user supplies a script, treat its premise, character relationships, beat order, key visual moments, and ending as the base route. Variants must preserve the requested core while changing meaningful story structure. Optional libraries may inspire alternatives but never replace the base route.

When the user requests variants without a selected script, use the stated count; use 20 only if they do not give one. Every variant must change at least two structural dimensions, and high-density variants at least three: opening event, character goal, relationship, pressure, setting, product-proof order, turn, outcome, camera logic, or emotional ending. Adjective swaps do not count.

## Route template

| Field | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Mode | State the route and why it fits the current product. |
| Buyer moment | A plausible adult context when story or lifestyle is used. |
| Tension or goal | An ordinary, evidence-compatible reason the product enters the scene. |
| Product proof | One visible action or detail supporting a confirmed fact. |
| Detail evidence | A time-coded macro/close proof shot, sharp and unobscured, with product coverage at least 60% (70% for fine detail). |
| Turn | A discovery, choice, reaction, or result that advances the story. |
| Ending | A stable product-visible final frame or explicitly requested alternative. |

## Route forms by duration

### 25-second high-density finished story

Use one causal spine and 1–3-second microbeats: hook → pressure → decision → product-relevant action → turn → proof → payoff. Select the model before deciding whether this is one continuous source clip or linked model-supported clips totalling 25 seconds. Do not make a montage of unrelated product poses.

### 30-second linked story

For 3 × 10 seconds, use setup/reveal → change/proof → outcome/payoff. State every handoff: actor pose, product position, setting, lighting, framing, and next action. Use accepted prior frames when the generation route supports them.

For 6 × 5 seconds, use short independent cuts only when product handling or fine details need tighter control. Each clip must still serve the same story rather than becoming an unrelated mini-video.

## Claims and audience

- Keep product claims and actions grounded in the current product evidence.
- Default to adults unless the brief establishes a compliant adult purchaser/participant context.
- Do not invent price, stock, certification, safety features, celebrity endorsement, or medical/therapeutic effects.
- Preserve a user-confirmed feature; if it is ambiguous or unsupported, identify the exact missing evidence instead of inventing it.
