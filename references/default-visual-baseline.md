# Default visual baseline

Use this baseline for TikTok Shop product generation unless the supplied material, product category, target market, or user brief calls for another direction. It is a quality floor, not a claim about the product and not a substitute for product evidence.

## Look

- Format: vertical 9:16, realistic smartphone UGC product footage; no simulated commercial logo, watermark, or readable UI.
- Light: bright, soft diffused daylight from one 45-degree side; clean neutral fill; soft natural shadows; accurate white balance. Preserve material-specific detail: controlled edge highlights for translucent/glossy objects, texture visibility for matte objects, and no blown highlights.
- Setting: choose a tidy, plausible, unbranded environment that supports the selected content intent. For product display, use a controlled surface/room that makes detail easy to read. For promotional or story-led work, prefer a specific real-life context, human moment, or visual hook over a generic tabletop; the setting must still leave the product legible and must not imply an unverified use. Use the supplied setting when it is clear and appropriate.
- Color: true-to-reference product colors, moderate saturation, no cinematic color cast, no artificial glow, and no unrelated accent color that could be mistaken for a product part.

## Camera and composition

- Use one deliberate camera move per clip at most: locked tripod-like shot, slow 5–10% push-in, or a short controlled overhead-to-detail move. Do not orbit, whip-pan, jump cut, or change focal length during a fine hand action.
- Hero/detail beats: product occupies 60% or more of the frame; use 70% or more when a small component, texture, or pairing must be verified. Keep the required component unobscured.
- Action beats: show both the acting hand and the full relevant product; use an adult hand unless the brief authorizes otherwise. Keep fingers, contact points, and start/end states inside frame.
- End every generated clip with at least one second of a sharp, stable, product-visible composition suitable as the next reference frame.

## Prompt shape

Write each clip in this order:

1. **Visual baseline:** format, realism, lighting, setting, and camera framing.
2. **Identity lock:** exact reference product, material, colors, pairings, scale, and forbidden substitutions.
3. **One action:** explicit start state, one visible action, and required end state.
4. **Acceptance/negative gate:** only the errors most likely for the shot—changed product, mixed pairings, warped geometry, duplicate parts, malformed hands, prohibited target, or prohibited text/brand/price.

Avoid stacking generic adjectives such as “high quality,” “amazing,” “cinematic,” or “professional.” They do not replace a concrete camera, lighting, state, and evidence specification.

## Override rule

Change this baseline when the product needs a different evidence-appropriate look—for example: a bright outdoor demonstration, a bathroom routine, a kitchen workflow, a studio macro texture inspection, or authentic supplied UGC footage. Record the override and its reason in the job/render manifest.

## Creative-default rule

When the user selects promotional/story-led work or does not constrain the creative direction, raise creative variety by default: make the setting, visual hook, and payoff materially different across scene blocks while maintaining the product identity lock. Do not turn this into a fixed plot or require actors when a visual concept is stronger. Ask for the content intent before choosing between proof-first product display and story-first promotion unless the user has already supplied a script, storyboard, or unambiguous direction.
