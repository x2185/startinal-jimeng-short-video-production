# Fast UGC payment-proof story skeleton

Use this structure only when the seller has confirmed that the exact SKU is a novelty cardholder intended to carry a user's own tap-enabled card and supplied product identity evidence. The holder is not a payment instrument and does not itself process a transaction. This is a case-specific optional pattern, not the default for future product videos. It derives reusable pacing from prior successful creative work; it never authorizes reusing a prior video, actor, location, brand, exact dialogue, or transaction claim.

## Reusable dramatic engine

`unusual object → companion disbelief → playful commitment → payment suspense → verified result → shared, low-key payoff`.

For a product that does not fit this route, reuse only the abstract pacing logic—an immediate visual question, a believable social reaction, a decisive product-relevant beat, a turn, and a low-key payoff—and derive every concrete action from the new product's own evidence.

The joke is the contrast between the holder's unexpected appearance and an ordinary payment setting. Keep the performance conversational and understated. The product is a real plastic cardholder, not a magical prop or payment device: no glow, floating, visual effects, fantasy powers, or claims that it processes payment.

## 15-second proof pattern

Use this as a compact proof beat inside a longer continuous story, or as a standalone short route:

| Time | Story beat | Visual/action requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3s | Hook | Show the exact product close to camera long enough to read; a companion reacts with mild disbelief. |
| 3–5s | Commitment | The holder makes a playful, credible decision to test it; preserve the product in a stable visible hand position. |
| 5–7s | Arrive at proof moment | Cut directly to the ready-to-pay state; omit ordering and other setup. |
| 7–10s | Suspense | Show one attempted tap in a clean close shot. Do not expose card, account, customer, or merchant data. |
| 10–12s | Result | Show a natural payment-success cue, then cut to the pair's restrained reaction. Keep terminal and customer information unreadable. |
| 12–15s | Payoff | Return to the pair walking away or leaving the counter; end on a natural smile and product-visible close frame. |

For a 25-second high-density story, add a relatable setup and an earned reaction around this spine. Retain a single causal chain rather than treating several unrelated checkout locations as evidence.

## Controlled variety

Vary the setting, relationship, hook wording, camera distance, reaction order, and payoff. Keep the following fixed:

- product identity is locked with same-SKU reference images;
- dialogue is short and conversational, with no copied wording from prior videos;
- no merchant logo, price, card number, customer information, or readable terminal data;
- the tap is the only fine interaction in that beat; and
- the final product close-up has a stable composition.

Rapid 0.3-second multi-location payment montage is a style beat, not a detailed demonstration. Keep logos, terminal data, cards, and customer information unreadable, and do not imply several separate verified transactions.

## Evidence and safety gate

Before a paid run, require all of these:

1. Seller confirmation that the exact SKU is the shown cardholder configuration and can carry the user's own contactless card as depicted.
2. A clean identity image plus start, contact, and end-state reference material for the holder interaction.
3. Confirmation that any real location, actor, screen, and data are cleared and that terminal/card information is not visible.

If holder-configuration confirmation or sufficient product-action references are missing, retain the comedy and reaction structure but replace the unsupported payment beat with an ordinary product/lifestyle reveal. Keep terminal, card, account, and customer information unreadable.
