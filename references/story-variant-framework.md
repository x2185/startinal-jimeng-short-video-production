# Story-led variant framework

## Derive, do not copy

Generate routes from the current product brief. Do not carry over example stories, specific props, characters, or product actions from previous jobs. Use only confirmed claims and visible/filmable proof.

## Route template

For each of five routes, specify the full 30-second story:

| Field | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Buyer moment | A plausible adult context relevant to the target buyer. |
| Tension or goal | An ordinary, non-medical reason the product enters the scene. |
| Product proof | A visible action that supports one confirmed product fact. |
| Turn | The discovery, choice, reaction, or transformation that advances the scene. |
| Ending | A stable product-visible frame that can cut into the next clip or CTA. |

Do not treat a pretty background, camera angle, or new adjective as a route.

## Four complete 30-second variants per route

Keep the route's core buyer moment, then vary at least two structural dimensions. Useful dimensions include:

- first-frame event;
- actor objective;
- setting and props;
- product action and proof sequence;
- solo versus adult-to-adult relationship;
- reveal mechanism;
- outcome;
- camera viewpoint;
- CTA-safe frame placement.

Record which dimensions changed. If fewer than two changed, revise the variant.

The default deliverable is 5 routes × 4 variants = **20 complete 30-second story packages**. With a 3 × 10-second JiMeng limit, output exactly **three linked prompts per package: 60 prompts total**. Keep the three prompts grouped under their package ID; never pool or reorder them across packages.

## Three linked 10-second clips for one 30-second story

Use this timing as a guide:

Write every matrix cell as a full story package, never as an isolated 10-second idea:

| Clip | 30-second story role | Required handoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1, 0–10s | Setup and product reveal | State the stable final product position, actor pose/hand, setting, framing, lighting, and direction of action. |
| 2, 10–20s | Continue the same moment and show proof/escalation | Begin by repeating clip 1's final state. Use clip 1's accepted final frame as an additional reference when available. State its own stable ending. |
| 3, 20–30s | Resolve the story and create product hero/CTA frame | Begin by repeating clip 2's final state. Use clip 2's accepted final frame as an additional reference when available. End with stable CTA-safe product composition. |

Keep the same product, recurring adult actor, wardrobe, location, lighting, time of day, and camera logic across all three prompts unless a visible in-story transition is explicitly planned. Each prompt must say both what it inherits and what it hands off. A 10-second clip may have an internal mini-arc, but it serves the single 30-second narrative.

## Claim and audience guardrails

- A subjective “satisfying” or “pleasant tactile break” can be shown as an actor reaction; do not claim treatment, diagnosis, or guaranteed emotional outcome.
- Default to adults unless the brief explicitly establishes a compliant adult purchaser/participant context.
- Do not invent a testimonial, price, discount, stock level, materials, certification, age recommendation, or safety feature.
