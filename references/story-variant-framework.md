# Flexible toy creative framework

## Derive, do not copy

Generate routes from the current product brief. Do not carry over example stories, specific props, characters, or product actions from previous jobs. Use only confirmed claims and visible/filmable proof.

## Creative modes

Choose only modes the current toy and assets can genuinely support. The default 20-package set should mix modes instead of making all 20 story-led. Start from a balanced mix—story 4, gameplay/function proof 5, visual showcase 4, interaction/reaction 3, hook/reveal 2, gift/collection/lifestyle 2—then reallocate any unsupported mode to a supported one and record why.

| Mode | What it achieves | Use when | Do not do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Situation story | Creates emotional context and a clear before/after moment. | A believable adult context and payoff fit the product. | Invent a need, function, or dramatic event. |
| Gameplay/function proof | Makes the actual play action instantly understandable. | The toy has a confirmed action, rule, mechanism, or assembly result. | Add actions the supplied toy cannot do. |
| Visual product showcase | Sells shape, material, colors, components, and satisfying visual detail. | The physical appearance itself is a strength. | Pad 30 seconds with static rotations or slow motion. |
| Interaction/reaction | Shows turn-taking, surprise, comparison, or social play. | Two adult actors can demonstrate a real interaction. | Claim fake testimonials or target purchase persuasion at children. |
| Hook/reveal | Starts with a visual question, then resolves it through a real feature. | Appearance, transformation, or mechanism allows a truthful reveal. | Promise a hidden feature not confirmed by the brief. |
| Gift/collection/lifestyle | Places the toy in a real display, packing, giving, or collecting moment. | Packaging, collectability, décor value, or gifting is confirmed. | Invent limited editions, price, stock, or collectible rarity. |

## Route template

For each selected route, specify the full 30-second creative package:

| Field | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Mode | State the selected creative mode and why it fits this product. |
| Buyer moment | A plausible adult context relevant to the target buyer; required only for story/lifestyle modes. |
| Tension or goal | An ordinary, non-medical reason the product enters the scene; required only for story/challenge modes. |
| Product proof | A visible action that supports one confirmed product fact. |
| Detail evidence | At least one time-coded macro/close shot that proves named identity traits and/or a confirmed mechanism; product fills >=60% of the frame (>=70% for fine surface detail), in sharp focus and not obscured. |
| Turn | The discovery, choice, reaction, or transformation that advances the scene. |
| Ending | A stable product-visible frame that can cut into the next clip or CTA. |

Do not treat a pretty background, camera angle, or new adjective as a route. A showcase or demonstration is valid without a plot only when its hook, proof sequence, and final visual payoff are materially different from the other packages.

## Reusable toy-story route library

Use these as structural references for any toy product; select only routes supported by the product's confirmed play pattern, components, and target audience. Never copy a previous toy's action into a new product.

| Route | Story engine | Natural proof moment | 30-second payoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Curiosity reveal | An adult notices an unfamiliar object or an unexpected feature. | Reveal the actual mechanism, texture, transformation, or included part. | The discovery is understood and the toy is clearly presented. |
| Tiny challenge | An adult sets a simple, visible goal: complete, balance, match, launch, arrange, or solve. | Show the one real action or rule that advances the goal. | The goal is reached, nearly reached, or amusingly reset. |
| Make-and-test | An adult builds, assembles, changes, or prepares the toy, then tests it. | Close proof of the connection, moving part, result, or finished detail. | A stable view of the completed play result. |
| Shared reaction | Two adults take turns, guess, compare, or react to a toy's real behavior. | One person performs the feature while the other observes; use a short unobscured detail insert. | A visible shared response, then a clean product hero frame. |
| Everyday interruption | A mundane moment is interrupted by the toy: waiting, desk break, tidying, travel, or a gift moment. | Show the confirmed play action that changes the moment. | The moment resumes with the toy naturally retained in frame. |
| Surprise transformation | A toy changes state through a confirmed action: open/close, fold, reveal, light, move, stretch, stack, or switch. | Show before → action → after, with a short close proof of the changed part. | Hold the final state so the transformation is instantly understood. |

Select the best-fitting modes and routes for the current product. If the product has only one confirmed action, vary the surrounding goal, relationship, proof order, outcome, hook, and visual treatment rather than inventing unsupported functions.

## Balanced 20-package variation set

Within each selected mode and route, vary at least two structural dimensions. Useful dimensions include:

- first-frame event;
- actor objective;
- setting and props;
- product action and proof sequence;
- solo versus adult-to-adult relationship;
- reveal mechanism;
- outcome;
- camera viewpoint;
- CTA-safe frame placement.

Record which dimensions changed. If fewer than two changed, revise the variant.

The default deliverable is **20 complete 30-second creative packages** across the selected modes. With a 3 × 10-second JiMeng limit, output exactly **three linked prompts per package: 60 prompts total**. Keep the three prompts grouped under their package ID; never pool or reorder them across packages.

## Three linked 10-second clips for one 30-second story

Use this timing as a guide:

Write every matrix cell as a full creative package, never as an isolated 10-second idea:

| Clip | 30-second story role | Required handoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1, 0–10s | Hook/setup and product reveal | State the stable final product position, actor pose/hand when applicable, setting, framing, lighting, and direction of action. |
| 2, 10–20s | Continue with the decisive interaction and short proof/escalation | Begin by repeating clip 1's final state. Use clip 1's accepted final frame as an additional reference when available. State its own stable ending. |
| 3, 20–30s | Show the outcome/payoff and create product hero/CTA frame | Begin by repeating clip 2's final state. Use clip 2's accepted final frame as an additional reference when available. End with stable CTA-safe product composition. |

Keep the same product, and when an actor or scene is used, the recurring adult actor, wardrobe, location, lighting, time of day, and camera logic across all three prompts unless a visible transition is explicitly planned. Each prompt must say both what it inherits and what it hands off. A 10-second clip may have an internal mini-arc, but it serves the single 30-second package.

## Detail-evidence shot rule

Every 30-second package must reserve an uninterrupted 2-second product-detail shot, usually in clip 2 or clip 3. Name the exact traits to show; set frame coverage to at least 60% (70% for small markings, texture, seams, labels, or moving parts); require macro/close focus and stable lighting; and prohibit hands, props, captions, or motion blur from hiding the named traits. When demonstrating a mechanism, separate the close proof of the mechanism from the wide story shot; do not expect one distant shot to prove both. Keep it in real time unless a confirmed feature itself requires visible recovery; it is proof, not filler.

## Claim and audience guardrails

- A subjective “satisfying” or “pleasant tactile break” can be shown as an actor reaction; do not claim treatment, diagnosis, or guaranteed emotional outcome.
- Default to adults unless the brief explicitly establishes a compliant adult purchaser/participant context.
- Do not invent a testimonial, price, discount, stock level, materials, certification, age recommendation, or safety feature.
